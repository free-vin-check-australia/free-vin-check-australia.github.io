Stolen Vehicle Check Australia – What to Check

Learn how PPSR and state registration services can help identify available stolen-vehicle indicators before buying a used car.

Last updated 24 November 2026

Important Tasmanian exception: the PPSR states that stolen vehicle information is not available through its NEVDIS data for Tasmanian vehicles. Follow the Tasmanian Government’s own rego and vehicle-buying guidance as an additional check.

How to check whether a vehicle may be recorded as stolen

  1. Obtain the VIN or chassis number from the permanent vehicle marking and confirm it matches the paperwork.
  2. Run the official PPSR used vehicle search. The PPSR can return available NEVDIS indicators for stolen or written-off vehicles, but NEVDIS information is not always available or up to date.
  3. For a Tasmanian-registered vehicle, also use the Tasmanian Government vehicle-buying guidance and its registration lookup. The state advises buyers to check stolen and wrecked status through its own service.
  4. If a result indicates the vehicle may be stolen, do not confront the seller. Stop the purchase process and contact police or the relevant road authority using official contact details.

What a negative result does not mean

A search result without a stolen indicator is not a guarantee that a vehicle is not stolen. Information may be incomplete, delayed or recorded against a different identifier. Check the VIN physically, verify seller identity and authority to sell, inspect the vehicle and obtain current records at the time of purchase.

Do not confuse these checks

  • VIN validation: checks character format and selected VIN code segments.
  • Rego lookup: checks the registration record held by a state or territory.
  • PPSR search: checks security interests and displays available vehicle information from linked records.
  • Police records: are managed by police and relevant government agencies, not by this website.
