PPSR check explained

The PPSR is the government register that tells you if a car has finance owing. This guide covers what a PPSR check shows and how to run one.

Last updated 24 November 2026

Check a VIN now. Use the free VIN checker to validate a VIN and decode its country, maker and model year.

Tasmania: PPSR does not provide stolen-vehicle information for Tasmanian vehicles through its NEVDIS data. Use the Tasmanian Government registration and vehicle-buying checks as an additional step.

What is the PPSR?

The Personal Property Securities Register (PPSR) is an Australian Government register of security interests in personal property, including cars. Lenders record their interest in a vehicle against its VIN, so a buyer can search before paying.

What does a PPSR check show?

  • Security interest. Whether a lender has recorded an interest, which usually means money is owing on the vehicle. It does not show the amount owed or the ownership history.
  • Written-off status. Whether the vehicle is recorded as written off.
  • Stolen status. Whether the vehicle is recorded as stolen.
  • Vehicle description. Typically make, model and colour, so you can confirm it matches the car.
  • Search certificate. A certificate that serves as a legal record of the search result.

Why it matters

A private seller does not have to tell you that a loan is secured against the car. If the seller stops repaying and the lender has a recorded interest, the lender may be able to repossess the vehicle from the new owner. A check before payment is the simplest protection.

How to run a PPSR check

  1. Get the 17-character VIN, or the chassis number for most older vehicles. Check it first with our free VIN checker.
  2. Go to ppsr.gov.au/carcheck.
  3. Enter the VIN carefully. The fee is paid by card and is not refundable, so a mistyped VIN costs you the fee.
  4. Download and keep the search certificate.

You cannot search by number plate or engine number.

Is there a free PPSR check?

The official search charges a fee. Some businesses offer a PPSR certificate at no charge to the customer, usually in return for contact details. Third-party sites sometimes charge more than the official price for the same search, so compare with the official site before you pay.

PPSR check versus REVS check

REVS refers to the old state registers of encumbered vehicles. Since the PPSR started in 2012, those state searches have been replaced by the single national register. When people search for a "REVS check", the PPSR check is what they need today.

Limits of a PPSR check

  • Written-off and stolen data comes from state and territory road agencies and police via NEVDIS. It can be missing or out of date.
  • Stolen status is not covered for vehicles registered in Tasmania.
  • It does not show odometer history, service history or accident damage that was never reported.

For those, see the vehicle history check guide and get an independent inspection.

Frequently asked questions

What is a PPSR check?

A search of the national PPSR register using a VIN or chassis number. It shows whether a security interest is recorded and whether the vehicle is recorded as written off or stolen.

How much does a PPSR check cost?

The official search charges a fee paid by card. Check the PPSR site for the current price, because third-party sites can charge more.

Can I do a PPSR check with a number plate?

No. The official search needs a VIN or chassis number.

Is PPSR the same as REVS?

REVS was the old state-based search. The PPSR replaced it with one national register.

Does the PPSR show how much money is owing?

No. It only shows whether a security interest is recorded.
