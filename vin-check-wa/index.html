How to run a VIN check for Western Australia

Western Australian buyers often search for a "REVS check WA" or "VIN check WA". The old REVS (Register of Encumbered Vehicles) searches have been replaced by the national PPSR search. The VIN itself is national, so the same 17-character code works the same way in every state. Use the tool above to confirm the VIN is valid, then follow these steps.

  1. Find the VIN on the car and on the registration papers, and make sure they match.
  2. Enter the VIN in the checker to confirm the format, country, maker and model year code.
  3. Run a PPSR search with the VIN to see whether a security interest is recorded and whether the car is recorded as written off or stolen.
  4. Check the registration status with the WA Department of Transport using the number plate.
  5. Arrange an independent inspection before you pay.

What a WA buyer should know about PPSR and REVS

Before the PPSR began in 2012, each state ran its own register of encumbered vehicles, often called REVS. Those searches were replaced by the national Personal Property Securities Register. Today a single PPSR search covers a vehicle whichever state it is registered in, so a "REVS check WA" now means a PPSR check.

A PPSR search shows whether a lender has recorded a security interest against the VIN. It does not show how much is owed. If money is owing and the seller stops repaying, the lender may be able to repossess the car from the new owner. That is why a check before payment matters in Western Australia as much as anywhere else.

Written-off and stolen records. The PPSR may display available written-off and stolen indicators supplied through NEVDIS. NEVDIS data can be unavailable or out of date; stolen-vehicle information is not available through PPSR for Tasmanian vehicles. Use the relevant state checks as well as an inspection and the seller’s documents.

Official checks for Western Australia buyers

  • PPSR CarCheck: security interest, written-off and stolen status.
  • Register of Approved Vehicles: look up a VIN for vehicles supplied under the Road Vehicle Standards Act 2018.
  • Official WA Government registration check ↗: registration status and expiry information. Check the service’s own terms for the precise fields available.

A tip for WA buyers

WA buyers often buy from interstate, and long transport distances mean you may not see the car before paying. Insist on a clear photo of the windscreen VIN and the compliance plate, and run the PPSR search before any money moves.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free VIN check for Western Australia?

Yes. The checker on this page is free and works for a vehicle from any state, including Western Australia. It validates the VIN and decodes the country, maker and model year. For finance, write-off and stolen records, use the official PPSR search.

Is a REVS check the same as a PPSR check in WA?

REVS checks were the old state-based searches of Registers of Encumbered Vehicles. They have been replaced by the national PPSR search, which covers every state and territory.

Where do I find the VIN on a car registered in WA?

Look at the base of the windscreen on the driver side, the compliance plate in the engine bay or door pillar, and the registration papers. The VIN is the same Australia-wide.

Can I check WA rego with a VIN?

Registration status is held by the WA Department of Transport. Most state rego checks use the number plate. The PPSR search uses the VIN or chassis number, not the plate.

Does a VIN check show if the car is stolen?

A free VIN check does not. The PPSR search reports whether a vehicle is recorded as stolen. That data comes from state and territory agencies and police, so it can be missing or out of date, and stolen status is not covered for Tasmanian vehicles.
