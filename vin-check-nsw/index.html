How to run a VIN check for New South Wales

NSW is one of the busiest used car markets in Australia, so many buyers search for a "VIN check NSW" or "NSW VIN check" before inspecting a car. The VIN itself is national, so the same 17-character code works the same way in every state. Use the tool above to confirm the VIN is valid, then follow these steps.

  1. Find the VIN on the car and on the registration papers, and make sure they match.
  2. Enter the VIN in the checker to confirm the format, country, maker and model year code.
  3. Run a PPSR search with the VIN to see whether a security interest is recorded and whether the car is recorded as written off or stolen.
  4. Check the registration status with Service NSW and Transport for NSW using the number plate.
  5. Arrange an independent inspection before you pay.

What a NSW buyer should know about PPSR and REVS

Before the PPSR began in 2012, each state ran its own register of encumbered vehicles, often called REVS. Those searches were replaced by the national Personal Property Securities Register. Today a single PPSR search covers a vehicle whichever state it is registered in, so a "REVS check NSW" now means a PPSR check.

A PPSR search shows whether a lender has recorded a security interest against the VIN. It does not show how much is owed. If money is owing and the seller stops repaying, the lender may be able to repossess the car from the new owner. That is why a check before payment matters in New South Wales as much as anywhere else.

Written-off and stolen records. The PPSR shows written-off and stolen status from state and territory road agencies and police via NEVDIS. The data may be missing or out of date, so use it alongside an inspection and the seller's paperwork.

Official checks for New South Wales buyers

  • PPSR CarCheck: security interest, written-off and stolen status.
  • Register of Approved Vehicles: look up a VIN for vehicles supplied under the Road Vehicle Standards Act 2018.
  • Service NSW and Transport for NSW: registration status and transfer rules. Search the agency's name with "rego check".

A tip for NSW buyers

Many NSW listings are for cars first registered interstate. The VIN and the PPSR search are national, so one search covers a car whichever state it came from. If the vehicle has come from another state, also ask for its previous registration papers.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free VIN check for New South Wales?

Yes. The checker on this page is free and works for a vehicle from any state, including New South Wales. It validates the VIN and decodes the country, maker and model year. For finance, write-off and stolen records, use the official PPSR search.

Is a REVS check the same as a PPSR check in NSW?

REVS checks were the old state-based searches of Registers of Encumbered Vehicles. They have been replaced by the national PPSR search, which covers every state and territory.

Where do I find the VIN on a car registered in NSW?

Look at the base of the windscreen on the driver side, the compliance plate in the engine bay or door pillar, and the registration papers. The VIN is the same Australia-wide.

Can I check NSW rego with a VIN?

Registration status is held by Service NSW and Transport for NSW. Most state rego checks use the number plate. The PPSR search uses the VIN or chassis number, not the plate.

Does a VIN check show if the car is stolen?

A free VIN check does not. The PPSR search reports whether a vehicle is recorded as stolen. That data comes from state and territory agencies and police, so it can be missing or out of date, and stolen status is not covered for Tasmanian vehicles.
