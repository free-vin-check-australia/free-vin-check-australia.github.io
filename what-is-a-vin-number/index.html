What is a VIN number?

A VIN is the 17-character code that identifies one vehicle. This guide explains what each part means and what a VIN can and cannot tell you.

Last updated 24 November 2026

Check a VIN now. Use the free VIN checker to validate a VIN and decode its country, maker and model year.

VIN meaning

VIN stands for vehicle identification number. It is a unique code stamped or fixed to a vehicle when it is built, and it stays with that vehicle for life. Registration authorities, insurers, mechanics, recall programmes and history registers all use the VIN to refer to the exact vehicle rather than the model.

How long is a VIN?

A modern VIN has 17 characters, made up of digits and capital letters. The 17-character format was standardised from 1981. In Australia, Australian Design Rule 61 requires vehicles supplied to the market from 1 January 1989 to carry a 17-character VIN. Vehicles built earlier can have a shorter chassis number or frame number with a maker-specific format, and many Japanese vehicles use a frame number rather than a standard VIN.

The three sections of a VIN

World Manufacturer Identifier (positions 1 to 3)

The first character shows the region or country where the vehicle was built, for example 6 for Australia, J for Japan, K for South Korea, L for China, W for Germany and 1, 4 or 5 for the United States. The first three characters together identify the maker and vehicle type. For example 6G1 is a Holden built in Australia, JTD is a Toyota and WVW is a Volkswagen.

Vehicle Descriptor Section (positions 4 to 8)

These five characters describe the vehicle: the model, body style, engine, restraint system and similar attributes. The meaning of each character is set by the maker, so you need the maker's own key to read them fully. This is why a free decoder usually cannot tell you the exact trim level.

Check digit and Vehicle Identifier Section (positions 9 to 17)

Position 9 is the check digit. Position 10 is the model year code, position 11 is the plant code and positions 12 to 17 are the serial number that makes the vehicle unique.

Rules for Australian VINs

Australian Government guidance says a VIN is always 17 characters long, uses only the digits 0 to 9 and uppercase letters except I, O and Q, and ends with three numeric characters. The VIN database for every road vehicle in Australia is maintained under NEVDIS (the National Exchange of Vehicle and Driver Information System). Some older or foreign-market vehicles may not follow every rule.

Characters a VIN never uses

A VIN never contains the letters I, O or Q. They look too much like the numbers 1 and 0. If a VIN you have written down includes one of them, re-check the original.

What a VIN can tell you

  • Where the vehicle was built and by whom.
  • A broad description of the model, depending on the maker's codes.
  • The model year and plant, as coded by the maker.
  • Whether the number is well formed, using the format rules and the check digit.

What a VIN cannot tell you on its own

The VIN is only a key. It does not contain finance, accident, registration or odometer history. Those are held in registers and databases that you look up using the VIN. In Australia the main one for finance, write-off and stolen status is the PPSR, which you can search at ppsr.gov.au/carcheck. See our PPSR check guide.

Why decoding is not the same as checking history

Decoding reads the codes inside the number. Checking history searches databases for events linked to the number. A free VIN decoder does the first. A PPSR search or paid history report does the second.

Frequently asked questions

Is a VIN the same as a chassis number?

On vehicles with a 17-character VIN, the VIN is the chassis number. Older vehicles can have a shorter chassis number in a maker-specific format.

Can two cars have the same VIN?

Not within the same 30-year cycle. A duplicate VIN is a serious warning sign that a number has been copied or altered.

What does the tenth character of a VIN mean?

It is the model year code. The same code repeats every 30 years, so you confirm the year using the compliance plate.

What does the first character of a VIN mean?

It shows the region or country of manufacture. For example 6 is Australia and J is Japan.
